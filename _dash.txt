7. Critical Additions: Equipment, Blood, and Oxygen
The three systemic enablers that Department of State (DoS) implementing partners are required to integrate into their monitoring and investment scope under the EWENE Acceleration Plan and which are also recognized in the GHSD guidance as essential for reducing preventable deaths include;
Equipment Due Diligence: Before procuring or supporting procurement of medical equipment at supported facilities, IPs must verify: (1) facility readiness — power, space, and water quality; (2) existence of a maintenance contract and spare parts supply chain; (3) a sustained training and capacity-building plan that accounts for staff turnover; and (4) a tracking system to prevent diversion or loss. More than 8 in 10 devices in global studies did not meet product specifications before use, and 10–30% of donated equipment in LMICs never becomes operational. The guiding question is not "Can we buy this?" but "Are the conditions in place to make it work?"

Safe Blood Systems: Postpartum hemorrhage is the leading direct cause of maternal death in Kenya therefore it requires safe blood on the shelf at the moment of need. Twenty-six percent of PPH deaths are attributable to a lack of safe blood. DoS IPs should track blood availability at supported facilities, and advocate for SHA reimbursement of blood transfusion services. 

Oxygen Ecosystems: Respiratory distress syndrome contributes to approximately 45% of deaths among preterm infants in LMICs. Currently, only 1% of Level 3 and 20% of Level 4 facilities in Kenya have all requirements to provide oxygen and CPAP for neonates. DoS IPs should assess oxygen and CPAP availability and functionality at supported facilities particularly for small and sick newborns and flag gaps for county and national escalation. Investments in oxygen must be assessed against national readiness, system readiness (maintenance, workforce, monitoring), and clinical readiness (service packages, referral systems, respiratory care technologies).

8. Accountability and Governance
DoS IPs operate within the multi-level governance structure established under the EWENE Acceleration Plan and MNH RRI:
Facility level: Facility in-charges, MNH teams, MPDSR/QI committees, and sub-county health management teams.
County level: County Executive Committee Members, County Directors of Health, County Health Management Teams, and County RRI Technical Committees.
National and partner level: EWENE Delivery Unit (Ministry of Health), VTP Technical Working Groups, DoS IP coordination platforms, and national MPDSR review mechanisms.
Following the Presidential Launch of the EWENE Acceleration Plan and MNH RRI on 28 May 2026, the Ministry of Health Director General has formally requested all partners to: align technical and financial support with EWENE and RRI priorities; support high-impact interventions at national and county levels; actively participate in EWENE governance and review mechanisms; and support resource mobilization, capacity strengthening, and knowledge sharing.

10. Conclusion
This revised integrated monitoring and tracking framework strengthens the original concept by embedding three critical dimensions absent from the initial draft: safe blood system readiness, oxygen ecosystem integration, and equipment due diligence. These additions align DoS IP monitoring scope with the full breadth of the EWENE Acceleration Plan and GHSD guidance on smart, durable health investment in low-resource settings.
By linking facility, community, county, national, and partner reporting systems and by ensuring that investments in commodities, equipment, and technology are paired with the conditions required to make them work the framework will support timely action, improved service quality, and better outcomes for women, newborns, and mother baby pairs across Kenya's 26 high-burden counties.

Integrated Monitoring Framework
EWENE VTP & MNCH — Department of State Implementing Partners, Kenya
1. Purpose and Scope
This monitoring framework operationalizes the Integrated Monitoring and Tracking Framework for EWENE Indicators concept note. It provides DoS implementing partners (IPs) with a structured, results-oriented tool to track performance across five domains: PMTCT/VTP quality of care, EWENE 90:90:80:80 coverage, facility readiness and safe systems, MPDSR and accountability, and data systems functionality. The framework aligns with EWENE Pillar 4 (Data, Monitoring & Evaluation), the MNH RRI bi-weekly reporting cycle, and PEPFAR VTP reporting requirements.
2. Results Framework
Theory of Change Summary
If DoS IPs strengthen real-time monitoring of VTP/MNCH indicators, ensure facility readiness (blood, oxygen, commodities, equipment), and actively participate in EWENE and RRI governance and review mechanisms, then supported facilities will deliver higher-quality, uninterrupted care to HIV-positive pregnant and breastfeeding women, mother baby pairs, and HIV-exposed infants, resulting in reduced missed service opportunities, improved PMTCT outcomes, and a measurable contribution to Kenya's EWENE targets for maternal and neonatal mortality reduction.
